About Us

Green Valley Farms has been family-owned for three generations. We grow maize, cassava, and a small plot of Theobroma cacao — the scientific name for cocoa — alongside seasonal vegetables.

We follow organic growing practices wherever we can, and we sell directly to the local community rather than through middlemen. Our soil is what farmers here call "red earth" — iron-rich clay loam that drains well after the rains.

What We Grow

Our staple crops, in no particular order:

  • Maize
  • Cassava

  • Onions
  • Plantain
  • Cocoa
  • Groundnuts

Our Planting Calendar

Unlike the list above, this order matters — each step depends on the one before it:

  1. Clear and till the land (February–March)
  2. Sow seeds at the start of the rains (April)
  3. Weed and tend the growing crop (May–July)
  4. Harvest (August–September)
  5. Dry and store produce (September–October)

Harmattan Tip: when the dry, dusty Harmattan wind arrives (around December), we mulch young plants to keep moisture in the soil. It isn't part of the main planting calendar, but it's one of the first things we teach new farmhands.

Farming Terms

Intercropping
Growing two or more crops — such as maize and cassava — in the same field at once.
Fallow
Leaving a field unplanted for a season so the soil can recover.
Harmattan
The dry, dusty trade wind that blows across West Africa roughly from November to March.
